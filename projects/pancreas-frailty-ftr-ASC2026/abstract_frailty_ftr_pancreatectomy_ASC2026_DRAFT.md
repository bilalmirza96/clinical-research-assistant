## Abstract body (structured, four bolded headers)

**Introduction:** Frailty is an established predictor of mortality after pancreatectomy, yet whether that excess
mortality reflects a higher burden of postoperative complications or a diminished capacity to rescue patients
once complications occur remains incompletely defined. Failure to rescue, the death of a patient after a major
postoperative complication, isolates the second of these mechanisms and has emerged as a marker of the quality
of complication management rather than complication incidence. This study evaluates whether the association
between frailty and mortality after pancreatectomy is driven by an increased complication burden or by a
failure-to-rescue gap among frail patients.

**Methods:** We conducted a retrospective cohort study using the «20XX-20XX» NSQIP «Pancreatectomy-targeted /
general» Participant User File. All patients who underwent «pancreaticoduodenectomy and distal pancreatectomy»
were included. Frailty was quantified using the 5-item modified frailty index (mFI-5: functional dependence,
diabetes, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, congestive heart failure, and hypertension requiring
medication); patients were stratified as frail (mFI-5 «≥2») versus non-frail. The primary outcome was failure
to rescue, defined as 30-day mortality among patients who developed at least one major complication. Secondary
outcomes were the incidence of major complications and overall 30-day mortality. Multivariable logistic
regression was used to estimate the adjusted association between frailty and failure to rescue, controlling for
«age, sex, body mass index, American Society of Anesthesiologists class, albumin, operative approach, procedure
type, malignant indication, and «other covariates»». «Sensitivity analyses / E-value / alternative frailty
thresholds as performed.»

**Conclusions:** In this national cohort, the excess mortality associated with frailty after pancreatectomy was
