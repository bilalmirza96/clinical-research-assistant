**Objective:** Multimodal therapy has improved esophageal cancer survival, but whether these gains have been shared equitably by race is uncertain. We assessed whether the Black-White survival gap narrowed in the immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) era and, if not, whether residual disparity reflects tumor biology or inequitable access to multimodal care.

**Methods:** Matched retrospective cohort study across three platforms: SEER (n=32,820), the National Cancer Database (NCDB; n=114,633), and a multi-platform genomic dataset (133 self-reported Non-Hispanic Black [NHB] and 3,211 Non-Hispanic White [NHW] patients; AACR Project GENIE). Multivariable models adjusted for age, sex, diagnosis era, histology, stage, anatomic site, comorbidity, facility type, insurance, and area-level socioeconomic measures. Primary endpoints were verified by 1:1 propensity-score matching and inverse-probability weighting. Pre-specified analyses included multiple imputation, counterfactual decomposition and causal mediation analysis.

**Results:** Cancer-specific survival across the ICI era improved significantly for NHW patients (within-race Era 3 [2018-2022] vs Era 1 [2010-2014] SEER HR 0.87; 95% CI, 0.83-0.91; P<.001) but not for NHB patients (HR, 0.92; 95% CI, 0.82-1.03; P=.16); Overall survival showed similar asymmetrical pattern (within-race Era 3 vs Era 1 HR, 0.78 [Black] vs 0.68 [White]). Over 2010-2023, Black patients had worse cancer-specific (adjusted HR, 1.29; 95% CI, 1.23-1.36; propensity-matched, 1.25) and overall (adjusted HR, 1.14; 95% CI, 1.11-1.17) survival. Among Stage I-III patients, Black patients underwent cancer-directed surgery at about half the White rate (adjusted odds ratio [OR], 0.50), robust within every stage, histology and across socioeconomic adjustment, facility clustering, replication in SEER database, matched analysis (OR range, 0.48-0.61; E-value, 3.41). Surgery was recorded as not recommended substantially more often for Black patients at every stage, whereas documented refusal was rare in both groups, identifying the surgical-recommendation step as the largest measurable contributor. In causal mediation analysis, differential surgery receipt accounted for about half of the survival disparity (OS 50%; 95% CI, 36-65%, CSS 47%; 95% CI, 23-71%). Chemotherapy and radiation receipt were equitable, with modest adjusted differences after accounting for the higher squamous proportion among NHB patients. In Stage IV disease, NHB patients were less likely to receive ICI (10.7% vs 19.5%; adjusted OR, 0.73) despite a tumor profile suggesting greater ICI responsiveness, including higher TMB-High prevalence at the 16 mutations-per-megabase threshold (23.3% vs 10.4%; OR, 2.61; P<.001) and over-representation of squamous-cell histology (33.1% vs 9.2%; OR, 4.85; P<.001).

**Conclusions:** The Black-White esophageal cancer survival gap widened across the contemporary multimodal era. NHB patients were about half as likely to undergo curative-intent surgery, and the dominant measurable driver was the surgical-recommendation step rather than patient refusal. Chemotherapy and radiation receipt showed no comparable disparity, indicating a modality-specific gap rather than generalized undertreatment. Despite favorable tumor biology predicting greater ICI benefit, NHB patients received ICI less often than NHW patients. Equitable operability review, surgical referral, and ICI-trial inclusion of Black patients are the highest-leverage targets.
